# Limitless: three-minute submission voice-over

Read the quoted bullets as speaking pointers. Headings, screen directions, and sources are not spoken. Keep the voice-over at a natural pace; speed up only the app footage to 1.5×. Rehearse against the timestamps and leave brief pauses for the viewer to see each action.

## 1. Introduction, problem, and solution | 0:00–0:40

**On screen:** Team or travel footage, the two disability statistics, then the Limitless homepage.

- “When our teammate Sashwath tore an ankle ligament, he needed wheelchair assistance and rebooking. He still wanted to explore, but planning became much harder.”
- “According to the World Health Organization, 1.3 billion people experience significant disability. The CDC reports that nearly 29 percent of U.S. adults reported a disability in 2022.”
- “An accessible entrance alone is not enough. Travelers may also need manageable slopes, restrooms, captions, or places to rest.”
- “We built Limitless to plan trips around each person's needs, while keeping the excitement of travel at the center.”

## 2. App demo | 0:40–1:45

**On screen:** Play approximately 98 seconds of recorded app footage at 1.5× to fit this 65-second slot. Match each pointer to the action; pause the narration briefly while the trip loads. Label the prototype footage “Demo data.”

- **Open Plan your trip; select needs and interests.** “Let's plan a day in San Diego. We enter our access needs, interests, and preferred pace. Here, we're asking for routes without steps and regular breaks.”
- **Review the intake summary.** “Before planning, we review what Limitless understood, so we can catch anything it missed.”
- **Generate the trip; show the day tabs and stops.** “The planner checks places against those needs and builds an itinerary. We can explore each day and see the planned stops.”
- **Show scores and source-linked research.** “The Accessibility Score shows personal fit. The Verification Score shows confidence in the information. We can also read the supporting sources.”
- **Show Places we skipped.** “Places that don't fit are shown with a reason.”
- **Open Tell Limitless; submit fatigue feedback; show the proposal and Apply.** “If we get tired, we tell Limitless. It proposes changes while keeping completed stops and commitments. We review the changes before applying them.”

## 3. Credible data and our difference | 1:45–2:25

**On screen:** An official-source link, an unknown field, then a simple flow: access needs → requirement checks → itinerary → feedback.

- “Our prototype has 200 synthetic demo entries, with official-source research covering 42. That does not mean 42 fully verified places.”
- “We use venue and government sources. Missing information stays unknown. Our plan adds human reviews and traveler reports that create review tasks, rather than instantly changing trusted data.”
- “ChatGPT and Claude can help research a trip. Our focus is a dedicated workflow: checking individual access requirements, showing evidence and uncertainty, and adapting the itinerary during the trip.”
- “The data decides which places qualify. AI helps explain the plan.”

## 4. Three-year goal, revenue, and closing | 2:25–3:00

**On screen:** San Diego → selected California cities → West Coast. Show proposed prices with a “Pricing hypotheses” label.

- “Year one focuses on selected San Diego places. Year two adds selected California cities. Year three targets the West Coast, as demand and our review capacity grow.”
- “We plan to test 19 dollars per trip, 49 dollars for human-assisted planning, and 29 dollars a month for venue listings, alongside booking commissions and partnerships.”
- “Revenue must fund data reviews, technology, and support, including our goal of human-backed, round-the-clock help. Payments never influence either score.”
- “Limitless. Your world to discover. Your right to explore.”

## Recording notes — not spoken

- If Sashwath narrates, replace “our teammate Sashwath tore an ankle ligament” with “I tore an ankle ligament,” and use “I” in the rest of the opening pointer.

- Keep the comparison about the workflow; do not claim general AI tools cannot cite sources or that competitors have no accessibility features.
- The 1.5× speed applies to the screen recording, not the voice-over. Add captions to the final video and keep source text large enough to read.
